# Remaining Chapter 4 R1 environments — visual assessment only

2026-11-26. All five approved originals are preserved byte-for-byte. **No revised
environment artwork was made.** Recommendations below require Nate's direction;
they do not change selection status or authorize replacement.

## Recommendation

Prioritize the two dialogue backgrounds for material-by-material cluster work.
Keep the three main-scene compositions and make only localized improvements if
Nate wants them. None needs a new room design or additional decorative objects.
Do not run a global palette reduction across these five images.

The actual approved Bureau and Control Tower PNGs were compared at native
314×206 and exact nearest-neighbour 4×, alongside the approved Chapter 4 staff
lavatory. Chapter 2 achieves density through grouped window frames, foliage,
equipment and surface planes; it does not give every surface equal texture.
Those are the useful benchmarks, rather than colour counts alone.

## CH4-01 — Grand Bellevue lobby

**Recommendation: retain; optional localized refinement.**

At native size, the red carpet, paired doors, intrusive duct, podium, framed lot display,
flowers and small auctioneer form a readable hierarchy. The room and its humorous
institutional intrusion should remain intact. The improved duct volume and
chandelier lighting are successful; there is no reason to replace them.

At 4×, the long pale carpet scuffs read as repeated diagonal strokes rather than
irregular pile wear. Some plaster cracks and missing-finish patches have equally
hard emphasis across otherwise quiet wall areas. The podium's wood panels and
flower heads have isolated fine marks that could be grouped more clearly, but
their silhouettes already communicate the objects.

If revised, limit the pass to carpet wear at the bottom/centre, selected damaged
plaster edges and the podium/flower material accents. Use broken, uneven clusters
that follow foot traffic, joints and existing surfaces. Preserve the vanishing
lines, floor, doors, drapery, duct, light hierarchy, flowers and NPC pose. Do not
increase the NPC to portrait detail. Colour count: **2,542**, diagnostic only.

## CH4-04 — Grand Bellevue restroom

**Recommendation: localized fixture and NPC cluster cleanup; retain layout.**

The three basins, mirror, stall bank, open right stall and both working plumbers
remain recognizable at native size. Perspective and the absurd plumbing tableau
are sufficiently established. The broad floor is calm, which helps readability.

At 4×, basin rims consist of thin, repeated pale bands; apron fronts and supports
have less useful material separation than the Chapter 2 desk/equipment planes.
The plumbers' shirts, trousers and faces contain mottled tiny tone changes: that
texture competes with the silhouettes without adding legible anatomy. This is
a scene-scale problem, not a reason to add facial detail. The floor also has
weak contact shadows under the workers relative to its strongly drawn grout.

If revised, paint a few stronger ceramic rim/apron planes, faucet highlights and
bracket/contact shadows. Group each worker into readable skin, shirt and trouser
clusters, retaining their sizes, identities, gestures and kneeling positions.
Preserve all sinks, stalls, the exposed toilet, plumbing and signs; do not
recompose the room or add fixtures. Colour count: **2,524**, diagnostic only.

## CH4-05 — Grand Bellevue ballroom

**Recommendation: retain; optional floor and small-object material cleanup.**

The piano, stage, formal figures, organizer with clipboards, seated figure,
refreshment tables and chair-carrying workmen remain distinct at native size. The duct,
curtain, windows and green-grey wall establish continuity. The room communicates
an overmanaged wedding reception without needing more objects.

At 4×, many floorboard edges and similarly valued tan fragments compete
with the small figures. Several large pale angular wear patches read as isolated
shapes rather than wear following the boards. The white floral arrangements and foreground table
accents contain isolated bright marks that can look speckled. The back-wall
damage is graphically strong relative to its shallow material shading.

If revised, quiet selected floorboard interiors, let wear follow the boards and group a few floral/cloth
highlights, while retaining the board directions, furniture positions, table contents,
character silhouettes, clipboards, chair, doors, stage and lighting.
The organizer must remain the same auburn-haired, slate-suited character.
Do not turn scene figures into tiny portraits. Colour count: **3,669**,
diagnostic only.

## CH4-B2-03 — Restroom dialogue background

**Recommendation: first material-cluster refinement pilot.**

Native composition and material modeling are strong: convincing rounded basins,
brass taps and pipes, mirror depth, tiled walls, green stall doors and directional
floor reflections. Those successful forms should be preserved. It needs a change
in pixel treatment, not a loss of detail or another flat geometric sink redraw.

The 4× basin/tap crop shows the material mismatch most clearly. Many adjacent
pixels contain barely different cream, brass and green values; soft edge
transitions and fine surface variation enlarge into a reduced-painting finish.
Mirror wear and ceramic crazing disperse detail across too many small marks.
The image is richly modeled but does not use Chapter 2's deliberate clusters.

Start with the near basin, tap, apron and supporting pipe as one material group;
retain the bowl ellipse, thickness, rounding, grout perspective and cast shadow.
Paint compact ceramic ramps and selected grouped wear. Extend only if that pilot
preserves the existing volume and clarity. Then treat mirror wear, green painted
panels and floor reflections by surface, using the original geometry as the
reference. The overall mismatch spans most materials; a few edge pixels or a
global quantizer will not resolve it. Colour count: **49,487**, diagnostic only.

## CH4-B2-11 — Loading-dock dialogue background

**Recommendation: second material-cluster refinement pilot.**

Native staging is successful: piano/chandelier at left, stacked crates, paired
lifts, overhead equipment and a bright open service gate. Metal and wood read as
different materials, and the gate provides a coherent source of directional
light. Preserve the entire arrangement and all recognizable story objects.

At 4×, the floor reflection, rust around the lift bottoms, chipped plaster and
beam fasteners contain continuous fine variations. Piano keys and brass details
have softened transitions that do not match Chapter 2's more economical hard
clusters. The amount of wear is plausible; its pixel organization is the issue.

Pilot one lift threshold and adjacent floor patch. Group rust into damage along
seams and lower edges, distinguish metal planes with a concise ramp, and preserve
the door recess and floor vanishing lines. If successful, continue separately
through plaster, piano/brass and floor materials. Keep the piano, keys, crates,
chandelier, suspended hardware, both lifts, gate and light direction. Do not
rebuild the architecture or invent signage. Colour count: **83,600**,
diagnostic only.
