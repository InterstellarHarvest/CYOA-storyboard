## 2. Owner-approved visual direction

The Grand Bellevue Hotel should evoke the institutional absurdity of Terry Gilliam's *Brazil* (1985), particularly its restaurant scene.

The hotel is NOT genuinely luxurious.

It is a worn, malfunctioning institutional building cosmetically decorated to appear elegant.

Use:

- Intrusive silver ducts and awkward mechanical systems.
- Visible exposed plumbing and electrical infrastructure.
- Damaged walls concealed by drapes and inexpensive decorative panels.
- Wilting flowers, ornamental screens, faded carpets, and mismatched repairs.
- Dubious retrofuturistic technology interrupting formal hotel furnishings.
- Artificial elegance concealing inconvenient decay.
- Practical spaces dressed up for a wedding and auction.

Avoid clean luxury hotels, polished marble palaces, steampunk ornamentation, generic science-fiction corridors, excessive visual clutter, and elaborate machinery that obscures gameplay objects.

Create original compositions inspired by this visual language, not copies of film frames.

The protagonist wants a toilet. The wedding is incidental scenery, not a wedding-management quest.

## 3. Mandatory two-stage artwork workflow

Nate has approved the following production method.

### Stage A — Detailed visual reference

Generate a large, detailed image establishing:

- Architecture and perspective.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Characters and important objects.
- Lighting and colors.
- Machinery and practical structures.
- All relevant narrative clues.

Preserve this image unchanged as the detailed visual reference.

It is not the final game asset.

### Stage B — Independently reconstructed pixel art

Using that detailed image as a guide, create a NEW image directly at the appropriate logical pixel dimensions.

For main scenes, this is **314×206 pixels**.

Reconstruct the original composition deliberately, retaining as much meaningful detail as the pixel budget permits.

Redraw architecture, objects, lighting, colors, and environmental features into carefully controlled pixel clusters.

Simplify where necessary for clarity.

Do not merely resize, crop, sharpen, posterize, or palette-reduce the high-resolution image and declare it completed native-grid pixel art.

If the process relies on scaled source pixels rather than independent reconstruction, document the result honestly as source-derived.

The goal is a convincing native-grid recreation of the detailed reference.

### Stage C — Exact 4× presentation

Main scenes display at 1256×824 using nearest-neighbor scaling.

No interpolation, blurry enlargement, or faux-pixel overlays.

The 4× image is a presentation export, not the authored source.

## 4. Scene characters versus portraits

This distinction is critical.

**Main-scene NPCs must be simplified for the 314×206 logical grid.**

They should remain identifiable through:

- Clothing color and silhouette.
- Hair color and general shape.
- Distinctive accessories.
- Body proportions and posture.
- Important gestures or actions.

Do not attempt to fit detailed eyes, noses, mouths, hair strands, or complex facial expressions into tiny main-scene characters.

Do not increase NPC scale unnaturally just to accommodate facial detail.

The 564×278 portrait presentation carries the facial expression, personality, costume detail, and conversational acting.

### Mandatory reference: approved Reception

Inspect these exact canonical assets:

- `art/scenes/conference_hall/reception/reception_scene.png`
- `art/portraits/receptionist/reception_background.png`
- `art/portraits/receptionist/receptionist_idle.png`

Locate their approved source files and verify their actual dimensions.

Study how the small receptionist in the main scene relates to her detailed portrait.

They are clearly the same person, but the main-scene character is much simpler.

**Use this relationship as the character-detail benchmark throughout Chapter 4.**

Do not modify or regenerate these approved reception assets.

### Portrait contracts

- 564×278 pixels.
- Separately authored tighter camera view.
- Opaque environment background.
- Transparent foreground character where the layered portrait system applies.
- Maintain character identity across all poses.

Never enlarge a 314×206 main-scene NPC into a portrait.

Never shrink a detailed portrait into a main-scene character.
